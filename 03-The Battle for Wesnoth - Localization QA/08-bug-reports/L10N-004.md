# L10N-004 — Incorrect question punctuation and unnatural noun phrase in Spanish dialogue

## Summary

A Spanish dialogue line uses incorrect question punctuation around a vocative and omits the definite article before a specific plural noun phrase.

## Environment

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Game | The Battle for Wesnoth |
| Version | 1.18.8 |
| Campaign | A Tale of Two Brothers |
| Scenario | Campaign dialogue |
| Language | Spanish |
| Platform | Desktop |
| Test type | Localization / Linguistic QA |

## Location

Campaign dialogue spoken by a secondary unit.

The dialogue is attributed generically in the original campaign dialogue source rather than being associated with the unit's name.

## Steps to Reproduce

1. Start the campaign **A Tale of Two Brothers**.
2. Progress through the campaign until the relevant dialogue is triggered.
3. Observe the dialogue spoken by the unit when reporting the orcs' presence to the north.
4. Compare the Spanish localization with the original English dialogue.

## Actual Result

The Spanish localization displays:

> ¿Capitán, qué hacen orcos tan al sur?

## Original English

The corresponding English dialogue is:

> Captain, what are orcs doing this far south?

## Expected Result

The Spanish dialogue should use correct punctuation for the vocative and a natural noun phrase:

> Capitán, ¿qué hacen los orcos tan al sur?

The localization should preserve the meaning of the original while following standard Spanish punctuation and natural grammatical usage.

## Issue Details

### 1. Incorrect question punctuation

`Capitán` functions as a vocative and is not part of the interrogative clause.

Current:

> ¿Capitán, qué hacen...?

Expected:

> Capitán, ¿qué hacen...?

The opening question mark should therefore appear immediately before the interrogative clause.

### 2. Missing definite article

The phrase:

> qué hacen orcos tan al sur

is understandable but unnatural in this context.

The dialogue refers to the specific group of orcs observed by the scouts. In natural Spanish, the definite article is appropriate:

> qué hacen **los orcos** tan al sur

This issue results from transferring the article-less English plural structure directly into Spanish.

## Suggested Correction

> Capitán, ¿qué hacen los orcos tan al sur?

## Severity

**Minor**

The dialogue remains understandable and does not prevent progression or gameplay. However, the issue affects grammatical correctness, punctuation, and the naturalness of the Spanish localization.

## Priority

**Low**

The issue has limited functional impact but should be corrected as part of localization quality improvements.
